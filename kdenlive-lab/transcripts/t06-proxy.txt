Why proxies exist, measured on the lab's 4K clip
(2 seconds of 3840x2160 test footage).

A proxy at the size Kdenlive typically makes (640 wide):

$ ffmpeg -i media/big-4k.mp4 -vf scale=640:360 \
    -c:v libx264 media/big-4k-proxy.mp4

$ ls -l media/big-4k.mp4 media/big-4k-proxy.mp4
media/big-4k.mp4        4329480 bytes
media/big-4k-proxy.mp4    50375 bytes     (86x smaller)

Decode cost (ffmpeg reading every frame, nothing else):

$ time ffmpeg -i media/big-4k.mp4 -f null -
real  1.488s
$ time ffmpeg -i media/big-4k-proxy.mp4 -f null -
real  0.087s                              (about 17x less)

An editor decodes footage constantly -- every scrub,
every preview frame. The proxy is the same pictures at
a fraction of the decoding price, which is the whole
idea of chapter 13.
