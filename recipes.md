# Recipes

Small jobs, whole. Each assumes the
project of chapter 4, footage that is
yours (chapter 1), and a render check at
the end (chapter 10).

## The middle ten seconds, kept

A clip with one good stretch. Audition
it in the clip monitor, I at the start
of the good part, O at the end, drag the
marked stretch to the timeline, render.
Nothing else on the timeline, nothing
else needed — most "editing" requests
from family are exactly this.

## A talk, cleaned up

A recorded talk with dead air and two
coughs. Put the whole talk on the
timeline. Work through it in the project
monitor: cut before and after each dead
stretch with the razor (chapter 5),
delete, ripple closed. Bend the audio
level line down over the coughs
(chapter 6). Add one title at the start
with the speaker's name (chapter 7).
Render MP4. The talk is shorter, and
nobody can say where the cuts are.

## Photo slideshow with music

Stills are clips too: drag photographs
to a video track and each becomes a
clip of a few seconds (the length is a
setting; five seconds is kind). Add the
music track underneath. For life, apply
the Transform effect to each photo with
two keyframes — scale 100% at the
start, 108% at the end — the slow push
called the Ken Burns effect, which is a
zoom the audience feels rather than
sees. Dissolve between photos, half a
second each (chapter 8). Copyright
applies to the music exactly as chapter
1 says: yours, licensed, or written for
the purpose.

## Two cameras, one event

The school-play problem: two recordings
of the same event, and the sound is only
good on one. Put both clips on two video
tracks, aligned by their sound waveforms
(the loud clap at the start is the
landmark — nudge until the waveforms
match). Mute the bad camera's audio.
Then edit by hiding: cut the *top*
track and delete stretches where the
lower camera has the better view,
letting it show through. The sound
never changes, so the joins are
invisible.

## Split a long recording into episodes

A two-hour recording that wants to be
four videos. Do the whole edit once —
clean the sound, fix the colour — then
Save As four times (`show-part1` …
`part4`), and in each copy delete
everything outside that part's stretch
and render. The project file is cheap
(chapter 11); your finishing work is
done once, and each part renders
identically dressed.

## Rescue the too-quiet interview

The chapter-6 order, as a recipe:
normalise the interview clip to raise
its overall level; then ride the level
line by hand where the guest turns away
from the microphone; then bring the
room tone and music under it; export
and listen on the worst speakers you
own. If it survives laptop speakers, it
survives anything.
