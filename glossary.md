# Glossary

**Bin** — the project's shelf of raw
material: clips, sounds, pictures and
titles, held as references to files
(chapter 2).

**Bitrate** — how many bits per second a
codec may spend; the real quality dial
of a render (chapter 10).

**Checkerboard** — the grey pattern the
engine draws where a clip's media
cannot be read: a missing file made
visible (chapters 11 and 16).

**Clip** — one piece of media on the
timeline or in the bin; also the file
it came from, and context must decide.

**Codec** — the method picture or sound
was compressed with (H.264, VP9, AAC,
Opus); distinct from the container
(chapter 10).

**Container** — a video file's wrapper
and surname (MP4, WebM, MOV, MKV).

**Cut** — the plain join where one clip
ends and the next begins; also the act
of splitting a clip with the razor.

**Decibel (dB)** — the unit of sound
level; in digital files, 0 dBFS is the
ceiling and everything real is below
(chapter 6).

**Dissolve** — a transition where one
shot fades out as the next fades in,
made by overlapping clips (chapter 8).

**Effect** — an instruction pinned to a
clip or track (brighten, quieten, fade)
applied while it plays (chapter 8).

**Effect stack** — a clip's effects in
order, top to bottom (chapter 2).

**Fade** — a keyframed walk of
brightness or volume from or to nothing
(chapters 6 and 8).

**Frame rate** — frames per second
(fps); a project's heartbeat, set by
its profile (chapter 4).

**In / out point** — the frames where a
clip's used stretch starts and stops
inside its source (chapter 5).

**J-cut / L-cut** — edits where sound
and picture change at different
moments: next sound early (J), previous
sound lingering (L) (chapter 5).

**Keyframe** — a pin saying "the value
is this, at this frame"; effects walk
between pins (chapter 8).

**LUFS** — loudness measured as ears
hear it, averaged; the online-video
rule of thumb is near −14 LUFS
(chapter 6).

**melt** — MLT's command-line renderer:
the engine without the window, and
this guide's laboratory instrument
(chapters 3 and 10).

**MLT** — the media engine underneath
Kdenlive (and Shotcut): it decodes,
mixes and renders; Kdenlive is the
workbench on top (chapter 1).

**Monitor** — Kdenlive's two small
televisions: the clip monitor auditions
one raw clip; the project monitor
shows the film (chapter 2).

**Non-linear editor** — an editor where
any part can be changed in any order,
and nothing is final until the render
(chapter 1).

**Normalise** — raising a recording so
its loudest peak reaches a chosen
level; fixes overall level only
(chapter 6).

**Preset** — a named bundle of render
settings in the rendering dialog
(chapter 10).

**Profile** — a project's canvas: size,
frame rate and proportions, chosen
before editing (chapter 4).

**Proxy clip** — a small stand-in an
editor works with in place of big
footage, swapped back for the original
at render time (chapter 12).

**Razor tool** — the timeline tool that
splits a clip at the playhead
(chapter 5).

**Render** — building the finished
video file from the project's
instructions; the only place the film
exists as a film (chapter 10).

**Ripple** — trimming or deleting in a
way that pulls everything after it
closed (chapter 5).

**Scopes** — instruments (waveform,
histogram, vectorscope) that draw a
picture's brightness and colour as
graphs (chapters 2 and 9).

**Timeline** — where the film is
built: tracks running left to right,
time along the top (chapter 2).

**Track** — one horizontal lane of the
timeline; video tracks stack, audio
tracks add (chapter 2).

**Transcode** — re-encoding a file into
a different codec or shape, e.g.
variable frame rate into constant
(chapter 15).

**Transition** — the governed meeting
of two clips: a cut, a dissolve, a
wipe (chapter 8).

**Variable frame rate (VFR)** — footage
whose frame spacing changes with the
scene; phones record it, timelines
suffer it (chapter 15).

**Waveform** — the drawn shape of a
sound inside its clip; also a scope
that draws picture brightness
(chapters 6 and 9).
